# PicoClaw Community Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

PicoClaw started as a "self-bootstrapping" experiment — an AI agent helping to rebuild
its own runtime — and it grew into a community of people from very different
backgrounds: embedded tinkerers, Go developers, hardware hackers, students, and people
running the assistant on a $10 board somewhere with a slow connection. That mix is the
point, not a side effect.

Everyone who takes part in this project — maintainers, contributors, reviewers, and
people just asking questions — deserves to do so without being harassed, belittled, or
made to feel unwelcome. We commit to keeping PicoClaw a place where curiosity is safe,
mistakes are expected, and help is easy to ask for.

This applies to you regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability,
neurotype, ethnicity, nationality, spoken language, level of experience, education,
socio-economic situation, appearance, gender identity or expression, sex
characteristics, religion, or sexual orientation.

## What We Expect

Behavior that keeps this community healthy:

- Assume good intent and ask before assuming. Many participants write in a second (or
  third) language; unclear wording is usually not a personal attack.
- Give feedback on the code, the design, or the idea — not on the person who wrote it.
- Accept a "no" or a "not now" gracefully. Not every feature fits the project's goal of
  staying tiny enough to run in tens of megabytes of RAM.
- Own your mistakes, say sorry when warranted, and move on.
- Be patient with newcomers. "Have you read the docs?" is fine; making someone feel
  stupid for asking is not.
- Respect maintainers' time. They are volunteers, and PicoClaw has no support contract.

Behavior that is not acceptable:

- Insults, put-downs, slurs, or personal and political attacks of any kind.
- Sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted attention.
- Harassment in public or in private, including repeated contact after being asked to
  stop.
- Publishing someone's private information — home address, work email, chat logs —
  without their clear permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, spamming, or sabotaging issues and pull requests.
- Anything a reasonable person in a professional setting would consider out of line.

## Maintainer Responsibilities

Maintainers are the ones who keep the project's spaces usable. They may edit, lock, or
remove comments, issues, commits, pull requests, or other contributions that break this
code, and they may close a discussion that has turned unproductive.

Moderation should be proportionate and explained when it is practical to do so. A
maintainer is not required to justify every decision to someone who is actively
disrupting a thread.

## Where This Applies

This code covers every space the project uses: the GitHub repository (issues, pull
requests, discussions, review comments), the Discord and other community channels, and
any event where PicoClaw is represented. It also applies when you are speaking for the
project in public — for example from an official account or while acting as a
representative at a meetup.

## Reporting

If you are on the receiving end of unacceptable behavior, or you see it happen, report
it to the maintainers. The current maintainer is [@afjcjsbx](https://github.com/afjcjsbx),
reachable through their GitHub profile. If a report concerns a maintainer directly, or
you are not comfortable using GitHub, use a private channel on the community Discord.

Please include what happened, where, who was involved, and any links or screenshots if
you have them. Reports are handled privately. Maintainers will not reveal the identity
of a reporter without that person's consent, except where required to keep someone safe.
There is no guaranteed response time — this is a volunteer project — but reports will be
looked at.

## Consequences

When deciding what to do about a violation, maintainers consider the impact on the
community and whether the behavior is a one-off or a pattern. Depending on severity,
they may take one of these steps:

1. **A conversation.** For mild cases — sharp wording, a rude comment, a heated thread —
   a private note explaining what crossed the line and what to do differently. A public
   apology may be asked for.
2. **A formal warning.** For a single serious incident or a repeated pattern, a warning
   with clear terms. During the warning period, you may be asked to avoid contact with
   the people involved, including the maintainers handling the report.
3. **A temporary ban.** For sustained or serious behavior, a temporary removal from
   project spaces for a set period. Contact with those involved is not allowed during
   this time.
4. **A permanent ban.** For harassment, threats, or a continued pattern of violations
   after warnings, a permanent removal from all project spaces.

A ban applies to the person, not to sidestepping it under another account or handle.

## A Note on AI-Generated Contributions

PicoClaw is co-developed with AI assistance, and AI-assisted contributions are welcome.
Responsibility for a contribution always sits with the human who submits it: you are
expected to understand what you are sending and to follow this code yourself. Using a
model to write abusive, deceptive, or spam contributions is no different from writing
them by hand.
